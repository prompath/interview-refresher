# Hiring

## In one minute

I hire against a written description of what the person must do in their first year, not a
list of tools. The interview loop tests each needed ability once, with the same questions
and scoring guide for every candidate, so the decision rests on evidence instead of
impressions.

## Key ideas

- **Define the role first.** Outcomes for the first 6 to 12 months; the gap in the
  current team; must-haves versus things that can be learned.
- **What I look for by level.**
  - *Junior*: fundamentals, learning speed, care about correctness.
  - *Mid*: delivers a defined project independently.
  - *Senior*: frames ambiguous problems, judgement, raises others' work.
  - *Lead*: sets direction, communicates with the business, grows people.
- **A structured loop.** Each stage has one purpose:
  1. Screen: motivation, communication, basics.
  2. Technical: statistics and machine learning reasoning; SQL or coding on realistic
     data.
  3. Case: framing an open business problem, measurement plan.
  4. Past work deep dive: what *they* did, decisions and trade-offs.
  5. Behavioural: collaboration, handling disagreement, ownership.
- **Take-home versus live.** Take-home shows real work but costs candidates time and
  favours those with free evenings; keep it under a few hours or use a live exercise on
  a prepared dataset. Discuss the result with them either way.
- **Scoring.** Written rubric per stage; interviewers score independently before
  discussing; evidence quoted, not "good feeling".
- **Reducing bias.** Same questions, diverse panel, no weight on pedigree or polish
  unrelated to the job.
- **Selling.** Candidates assess us too: be clear about the work, the data, the problems
  and the growth path.
- **Signals I value.** Says "I don't know" and reasons from there; separates what they
  did from what the team did; checks their own results.
- **Warning signs.** Tool lists without decisions; no baseline or measurement in past
  projects; blames others.
- **After the offer.** Onboarding plan: first-week setup, a first small deliverable, a
  buddy.

## My evidence

No formal hiring responsibility yet; say so. Related: assessing students' capstone work
as a teaching assistant, and having been through loops on both good and bad designs.

## Likely questions

1. **How would you hire a data scientist for this team?** Role outcomes, loop, rubric.
2. **What separates a senior candidate?** Framing and judgement, shown in the case and
   deep dive.
3. **Take-home or live coding?** Trade-offs above; my default and why.
4. **Skills or potential?** Depends on the team's capacity to train; never compromise on
   integrity with data.
5. **You have not hired before. How will you learn?** Shadow experienced interviewers,
   use a rubric, review decisions afterwards.
