# Stakeholders and vendors

## In one minute

Stakeholder management is making sure the people who depend on the team's work know what
they will get, when, and how sure we are, and that they are heard when priorities are set.
With vendors, I am clear on what we are buying, keep data handling and measurement under
our control, and make sure knowledge stays in the team when the contract ends.

## Key ideas

- **Map the stakeholders.** Who decides, who uses the output, who is affected, who can
  block. Interest and influence set how often and how I engage each.
- **Set expectations early.** What data science can and cannot do, likely timelines,
  uncertainty of results. Most disappointment is unset expectations.
- **Regular rhythm.** Standing check-ins, written updates, demos; no surprises.
- **Translate both ways.** Business goals into measurable problems; results into
  decisions.
- **Conflict between teams.** Find the shared goal, put the disagreement in terms of
  data or a test where possible, agree who decides, escalate together if stuck rather
  than around each other.
- **Managing up.** Bring options and a recommendation, flag risks early, make the
  manager's decisions easy.
- **Vendor selection.**
  - Requirements and success criteria before demos.
  - Proof of concept on our data with our metric.
  - Total cost, integration effort, lock-in, exit terms.
  - Security and personal data handling reviewed.
- **Vendor oversight.**
  - Deliverables and acceptance criteria in the contract.
  - Our own independent measurement: a holdout the vendor does not control.
  - Data shared on a need basis, with agreed handling.
  - Regular reviews of method and results; challenge the method, not only the output.
  - Documentation and handover so the team can run or replace it.
- **Working relationship.** Vendors do better work when given context and quick
  answers; treat them as partners and hold them to the criteria.
- **Advisory role.** Giving a vendor input on existing models and executions, and
  deciding how data are handled, is real influence without ownership.

## My evidence

At AIS: advisory work with two vendors (input on current models and executions, decisions
on data handling); implementing the control group that measures a vendor's attribution
project; building the bucket assignment for another vendor's tests; scrum meetings and
answering the business team. At Sertis I was on the vendor side, which helps me see both.
Never name the vendors.

## Likely questions

1. **How do you manage a difficult stakeholder?** Understand their goal and pressure,
   agree criteria, keep commitments, write things down.
2. **How do you evaluate a vendor's claim of uplift?** Our own randomised holdout and
   our own revenue definition.
3. **Tell me about a disagreement with a vendor or partner.** A prepared story.
4. **How do you avoid dependence on a vendor?** Documentation, internal ownership of data
   and measurement, exit terms.
5. **Two business units want conflicting things from your team.** Shared criteria, a
   joint session, and escalation together if needed.
