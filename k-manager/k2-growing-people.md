# Growing people

## In one minute

A manager's output is the team's output, so my time goes to making each person more
effective: regular one-to-ones that are theirs, feedback that is specific and prompt,
work that stretches them, and clear expectations for their level. Underperformance is
addressed early and directly, with support.

## Key ideas

- **One-to-ones.** Weekly or fortnightly, their agenda first: blockers, growth,
  concerns. Status updates belong elsewhere. Take notes and follow up.
- **Feedback.** Situation, behaviour, impact: "in yesterday's review (situation) you
  presented the uplift without the interval (behaviour), so the team took it as certain
  (impact)". Soon after the event, in private for criticism, and far more often for
  good work than most managers manage.
- **Radical candour.** Care personally and challenge directly; avoiding hard feedback to
  be nice is the common failure.
- **Coaching versus directing.** Match style to the person's competence and confidence
  on *this* task: direct a beginner, coach someone capable but unsure, delegate to an
  expert.
- **Career growth.** A written ladder of expectations per level; a development plan with
  one or two focus areas; projects chosen to build them; sponsorship (putting their
  name forward), not only mentoring.
- **Delegation as development.** Hand over ownership of an outcome, accept that it will
  be done differently, stay available.
- **Motivation.** Autonomy, mastery, purpose; show how the work affects the business.
- **Underperformance.**
  1. Check that expectations were clear and the cause (skill, motivation, fit, personal
     circumstances).
  2. Say it plainly with examples.
  3. Agree specific goals, support and a timeline.
  4. Follow through, including a formal plan and exit if nothing changes. Delay is
     unfair to them and to the team.
- **High performers.** Need attention too: bigger scope, visibility, honest career talk.
- **Psychological safety.** People report mistakes and null results only where that is
  safe; the manager sets that by how they react.
- **Performance reviews.** No surprises: everything in a review was said earlier.

## My evidence

Teaching and assisting 200+ students; supporting students' capstone projects; mentoring
through code review and documentation. No direct reports yet.

## Likely questions

1. **How do you run a one-to-one?** Their agenda, growth, follow-up.
2. **Tell me about giving difficult feedback.** A prepared story, from teaching or peer
   review if needed.
3. **How would you handle an underperformer?** The four steps.
4. **How do you develop a junior into a senior?** Increasing ambiguity and ownership,
   with feedback on framing and judgement.
5. **How would your approach change managing former peers?** Be explicit about the new
   role, keep fairness visible, ask for their feedback.
