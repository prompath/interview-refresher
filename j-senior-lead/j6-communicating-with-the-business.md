# Communicating with the business

## In one minute

I lead with the conclusion and the decision it supports, then give the evidence and how
sure I am, in the listener's terms. Uncertainty is stated as a range and what each end
would mean for the decision. Bad news and null results go out promptly, with what we
learned and the next step.

## Key ideas

- **Conclusion first.** "The model-targeted group converted about 60% better than random
  targeting; we recommend keeping it" before any method.
- **So what.** Every chart and number tied to a decision: continue, stop, change, test
  further.
- **Uncertainty in plain words.** "Between X and Y; even the low end pays for the
  campaign" or "the range includes zero, so we cannot say it worked".
- **Absolute and relative together.** "+60%" and "from a to b per hundred customers".
- **Explaining methods by analogy.**
  - Control group: "the same kind of customers, left alone, to see what happens anyway".
  - Difference-in-differences: "we compare how much each group changed, not where it
    ended".
  - Significance: "how surprised we would be to see this if the campaign did nothing".
- **Null results.** "No detectable effect at this sample size" is a finding. State what
  size of effect was ruled out and what would be needed to detect smaller ones.
- **Pushing back.** Acknowledge the goal, state the risk of the request as asked, offer
  alternatives with trade-offs, let the owner decide. Not "no", but "yes, if" or "here is
  what that would cost".
- **Requests for a number to fit a story.** Report what the data show; offer the
  legitimate framings; decline the illegitimate ones.
- **Know the audience.** Executives: one slide, decision, money. Business owners:
  segments and actions. Technical peers: method and assumptions.
- **Written follow-up.** A short note after each discussion: decision, owner, date.
- **Regular rhythm.** Standing updates prevent surprises; raise risks early.
- **Charts.** One message per chart, title states the message, intervals shown.
- **Listening.** Feedback cases from the business are data about the model; investigate
  and report back.

## My evidence

Scrum meetings and answering the business team at AIS; internal reports on model
performance and uplift; tracing the business team's feedback cases to causes and
presenting the conclusion; client presentations at Sertis; teaching.

## Likely questions

1. **Explain an A/B test result to a non-technical manager.** Do it live, in three
   sentences.
2. **Tell me about delivering unwelcome results.** A prepared story (months with no
   significant uplift).
3. **A stakeholder insists on a rule you think hurts performance.** Quantify the effect,
   propose a test, respect their decision.
4. **How do you handle being asked for a deadline you cannot meet?** Options with scope
   and risk for each date.
5. **Lead-level follow-up: how do you build trust with a sceptical business team?**
   Deliver small things reliably, explain their own cases back to them, and be straight
   about uncertainty.

## Pitfalls

- Method first, conclusion last.
- Hiding uncertainty to sound confident.
