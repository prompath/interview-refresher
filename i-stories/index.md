# My own stories

Prompts only. The answers hold employer detail and stay out of this site; write them
somewhere private and rehearse them aloud.

## Format for every story

Situation (two sentences), task (what I was asked or decided to do), action (what *I*
did, with the reasoning), result (measured if possible, otherwise what changed), and what
I would do differently. Two minutes each.

For each one, be exact about my role: **designed**, **implemented**, **extended**,
**maintained** or **advised**.

## I1. One story per current-job bullet

- Uplift tracking and the revenue table: what was missing before, what I built, who uses
  it. I measured the model's uplift; I did not build the model.
- An A/B test I designed end to end: the question, the design choices, the outcome and
  the decision.
- The global control group: designed by a principal data scientist; I contributed to the
  design discussions and implemented it. Which design points did I influence?
- The daily bucket assignment job: from ad-hoc notebooks to one tested job; the defects
  found.
- The mid-year change in control group sampling: how I noticed, how I showed the groups
  were no longer comparable, how the measurement was revised.
- The recommender: one rule I added with the business team and how I verified it.
- Maintenance: a production issue I diagnosed and fixed.

## I2. The gas optimizer, from scoping to handover

Who asked, what planners did before, how I learned the constraints, the formulation, how
it was validated, how it was handed over, what happened next.

## I3. Working with vendors and the business team

- A disagreement over method or data handling: positions, how it was settled.
- A time I said no, or "not yet", to a request.
- Explaining a result nobody wanted to hear (no significant uplift).

## I4. A production bug

How it surfaced, how I traced it, the fix, what I changed so it cannot recur.

## I5. Career questions

- The career break: one or two plain sentences, then what I did to stay current.
- From chemical engineering and shift engineer to data science: why, and what carried
  over (process thinking, optimisation, working with operators).
- Teaching 200+ students: what it taught me about explaining.
- Why this role, why now, and why senior or lead.
- A failure and what I learned (the AutoML product with no customers is a candidate).

## Rules for all stories

- No vendor or consultant names, no absolute counts or revenue, no internal names.
- Uplift percentages are fine.
- "We" for team results, "I" for my actions.
